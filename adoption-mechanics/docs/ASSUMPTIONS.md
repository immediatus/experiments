# Assumptions

Each row: what the model assumes, where the input comes from, and what changes if it is wrong.

## Part 1: the human ledger

| Assumption | Source or status | If it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Signals are binary with bounded log-likelihood ratio | Model choice; bounded signals are what make cascades possible (Smith and Sørensen) | Unbounded signals end cascades eventually; T3's rates become upper bounds |
| Errors correlate through one shared factor, at a single rho | Model choice, the one-factor (Vasicek) model | Several factors give a floor per factor; the single-factor floor is the case of one dominant shared background; the factor is normal, with no tail dependence, so errors that coincide more on the hardest questions would raise the floor above the computed one |
| Precision 0.7 and correlation 0.25 for the running case | Calibrated against peer review (T22): reliability 0.34 implies 0.70; the NeurIPS committees' disagreement implies a within-review error correlation of 0.23 to 0.32 | Peer review is an analogue of design review (Fit); T23 reruns the case at 0.67 and 0.28, and T10 sweeps both |
| Symmetric precision and symmetric herding | Model choice; the field evidence says otherwise: reviews spot weak proposals better, and experts follow critical scores more readily (Cortes and Lawrence; Lane and coauthors) | T23 adds both asymmetries; the review becomes more cautious and the conclusions hold |
| A member follows the lead once it exceeds their own signal by more than one step | Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch's rule, myopic | People herd less when observed (March and Ziegelmeyer); T3 adds that as a probing weight |
| Career plane: value of accuracy 0.25, endorsement weight 1.5 | Illustrative constants, swept in T4 | The sign of the endorsed senior's margin turns at T4's swept values; the post reports the region, not a point |
| Groups holding unequal evidence (T26): a pilot team of 5 at precision 0.8 and two groups of 5 at 0.65, deciding apart; a design any group does not favor stops | Illustrative; the mean precision matches the calibrated 0.70, and T26 also splits the evidence 0.70 / 0.70 / 0.70 and 0.90 / 0.60 / 0.60 | How real groups divide evidence is not measured |
| Cross-communication (T27): one owner settles the pairwise disagreements of consulted groups one at a time, each taking an exponential time, within a 13-week window | Illustrative; the mean settling time is swept at 0.5, 1 and 2 weeks | Parallel settling would shorten the time; the count of pairs still grows as the square of the groups |
| Conclusions carried between groups as a public lead of 1 or 2 signals each | Illustrative | How much a group weighs another's conclusion is not measured |
| Extra groups' ideal points between the status quo and the approvers' centroid, with scatter 0.25, 0.5 or 1.0 | Illustrative: a group pulled in late partly favors what it has | The design's chance falls similarly at every scatter swept |
| The law of triviality (T27): each reviewer holds an opinion on a piece with chance 0.2 times the number of pieces to a steepness of 0.5, 0.75 or 1.0 (0 as the control); pairs of holders settle in a quarter of a week each, one at a time; each holder accepts a piece with chance 0.9 | Illustrative, the steepness swept | How the share of opinion holders grows as pieces get simpler is not measured |
| Site reliability's release-readiness check | Not modeled: every approved design ships | A release check would add a stage after the approvals |
| Partitioned groups share the whole correlation | Model choice for the headline numbers | Part of reviewers' shared error is local to their room; T10 also gives divergence with 0.12 of the 0.25 local to each group, which is higher |
| Gates read the tally at face value | Model choice: the actuator compares the net share of endorsements, not its evidential weight, with the threshold | A sequence inflates the tally (T10), which is why a blind poll alone looks cautious |
| Each reporting layer keeps 0.85 of the signal's amplitude | Illustrative, swept in T10 | Adoption at 0.75 and 0.95 is in T10 |
| Gates' ideal points in two dimensions of concern, Euclidean preferences | Model choice; the hull form of Tsebelis's result needs circular indifference curves | With other strictly convex preferences (weighted concerns) the cliff is the gates' Pareto set, which can differ from the hull of the ideal points |
| Hysteresis: merit follows a random walk reviewed at discrete steps; switching cost and loss aversion set the band | Dixit's model, solved by value iteration | The band's exponent depends on the cadence (T6); the post quotes no single exponent |
| Loss aversion 2.25, weighting the periods in which the successor trails the incumbent | Tversky and Kahneman's estimate; placement is a model choice | Placed on the cost of leaving the status quo instead (status-quo bias), it raises the adoption threshold, 0.1771 to 0.2681 at a cost of 0.2 (T6); the running case uses the first placement |
| Hand-off as deterministic attenuation of the verdict against a fixed threshold | Model choice | It lowers adoption of both designs (T10); a hand-off that adds noise per layer would also blur them |
| Stated reasons: a fraction phi follow the true driver, the rest a story biased toward the incumbent | Model choice; human evidence from choice blindness, agent evidence from chain-of-thought faithfulness | A bias that varies across episodes moves the limit but not the existence of a wrong fixed point |

## Part 2: agents and mixed teams

| Assumption | Source or status | If it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| A benchmark question stands for a review decision | Fit; benchmark items are not design reviews | The measured rho describes models on MMLU; the transfer to reviews is stated as Fit |
| Latent correlation from the tetrachoric correlation of a pair's 2x2 counts | Standard estimator; assumes a bivariate normal latent | phi on the indicators is reported beside it, and is bounded by the Fréchet-Hoeffding limits when accuracies differ |
| Within-difficulty dependence read against a null | An item-response null with one difficulty per question and one ability per model (T25) gives the estimator's own artifact, about 1.15 | A null with a discrimination per question could absorb part of the strongest pairs' residual; it needs per-question records, which are not stored |
| Members predict the crowd from their own answer in the surprisingly-popular rule | Prelec, Seung and McCoy's specification | A static prediction (the earlier version) gave a slightly smaller gap; the dilution holds either way |
| Pooled dependence over questions of varying difficulty | Pooled is the quantity that sets a panel's floor over a question mix; difficulty is the shared factor | Within ten difficulty strata (difficulty from the other models) the Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio is near 1 for most pairs and above 1 for the strongest; strata leave some difficulty inside, so five strata read higher (T13) and an item-response model would refine both |
| Within-stratum dependence by the Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio | Standard for sparse stratified 2x2 tables; a tetrachoric correlation per stratum fails when a stratum has no shared error, which half the strata of the strongest pairs do | Pairs whose pooled odds ratio is not finite are dropped (a handful per benchmark) |
| Three benchmarks, one run per model, chain-of-thought scoring on HELM Capabilities | Data choice; HELM's latest release per model | Another release or decoding setting may move individual pairs; the counts are stored, so the computation can be repeated |
| One run per model and subject, the latest in the HELM Lite bucket | Data choice | Another run of the same model may differ by sampling; counts are stored, so the computation can be repeated |
| Tiers of equal size among models above half accuracy | Analysis choice; the upgrade condition requires p below one half | Other splits are computable from the stored counts |
| Agents in the mixed panel answer the successor with probability 0.3 when blind to the local fact, and 0.3 plus or minus half of agent_info when they carry part of it, correlated at 0.3 or 0.6 | Illustrative: a shared blind spot, and a knob for partial knowledge | T15 reports both states of the local fact; T17 sweeps agent_info and finds where the whole panel beats the people |
| 40% of the people hold the local fact | Illustrative | The people's own gap grows with the informed share; T15 sweeps the number of people |

## Part 3: AI as an instrument for review

| Assumption | Source or status | If it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| A model uses a fact at relative position x with recall 0.9 minus a middle dip that grows with how much of its effective window the document fills; the advantage of the beginning fades from half the window to the window, the advantage of the end stays | The shape follows published measurements (Liu and coauthors; the 2025 study of biases near the window limit); the constants are illustrative, and T29 sweeps the depth of the dip from 0.2 to 0.4 | A model with flat recall gains nothing from parts on a local fact and still loses relational facts to them; the direction of both results holds while any dip remains |
| A document read in parts is read in separate calls, each part covering 1.25 / parts of it, and a fact covered by several parts is used if any part uses it | Model choice: independent calls, a fixed overlap of 0.25 | Calls that share a summary of the other parts recover some relational facts; T29 then understates what parts keep |
| A relational fact needs both of its positions in one part, with the product of their recalls | Model choice: the two halves of the fact are used independently | Retrieval that links the halves, through an index or cross-references, raises the parts' relational rate |
| An agreement mediator carries each group's conclusion to the next as a lead of 1 or 2 signals | The carried conclusions of T27, read as a mediator that builds consensus group by group | A mediator that weighs groups by their evidence is the difference mediator of the same section |
| A difference mediator has every member vote blind and pools the evidence, each member counted once or by the log-odds of their group's precision | Model choice: the reliability weights an informed decision maker would use; how a mediator would learn the precisions is not modeled | Weights estimated with error lie between the equal and the reliability rows |
| A mediator that sharpens disagreements halves or quarters the mean time to settle one pair | Illustrative; no measurement of AI-mediated settling exists | The window rows show the size the effect would need; the triviality rows show that no settling speed rescues a split design |
| A two-sided debate every member hears enters each member's evidence as a public lead of its log-odds, in signals; heard after the vote, it is added to the tally with that weight | Model choice, the cascade rule of Part 1 applied to one shared input; the accuracies 0.6, 0.75 and 0.9 bracket the range Khan and coauthors measured for judges with and without debate | Members who discount a shared input are captured only at a higher accuracy; the capture condition moves, its form does not |
| A single advocate is a public lead toward the design whatever its merit, of 1 or 2 signals | The champion of Part 1, made persuasive | More persuasive advocates capture the review sooner, the direction Khan and coauthors measured for consultancy |
| A model's use of a document is the share of the gain over answering without it that its reading keeps, (accuracy - closed book) / (oracle - closed book) | Calibration choice (T32), on Liu and coauthors' multi-document question answering | A reading below the closed-book accuracy counts as using nothing; with another baseline the depths change and the direction does not |
| The fitted curve is extrapolated to the shorter inputs of a part by Definition 7's scaling of the dip with the fill | Model choice; the measured dip deepens with length for GPT-3.5 and barely moves for Claude-1.3 and MPT-30B | Two nearly identical measured curves extrapolate to 0.46 and 0.66 for four parts (T32): the size of the gain from parts is loosely pinned, its direction holds for every model |
| A critic flags a worse design's flaw 0.85 of the time and a better design falsely 0.3 of the time; people verify a share of the false flags away | Illustrative, in the direction McAleese and coauthors measured (critics catch more bugs and invent more); T33 sweeps the verified share | A critic with fewer false flags needs less verification; the ordering of verified over unverified holds |
| Members tuned to stop agreeing put a probing weight of 0.5 to 2 log-odds on their own signal | The probing weight of Part 1, read as the effect of tuning away sycophancy (Wei and coauthors) | How far tuning moves the weight is not measured |
| In the region, a model that uses the decisive local fact reports it with accuracy 0.95 (0.75 in the robustness case), with the chance of using it from T29 at a full window; the debate is 0.75 accurate (0.9 in the robustness case) | Illustrative; T34 changes each alone | The widest gaps keep a verified critic and a debate heard first in every case tried |
| A member follows a shared input only when its log-odds outweigh the member's own signal by more than the probing weight, each group judging by its own precision | Proposition 7 applied member by member; an input can capture some groups of a review and not others | Members who defer to any input that sounds confident would be captured sooner, and the combining rule would stop mattering earlier |
| Proposition 6 is a large-panel limit: agents carry no information about the local fact, people answer independently of the agents, and the rule's threshold is the share expected when the successor is worse | Proposition 6 (T38); the simulated panel of 20 agents and 5 people lands within a point of the limit | Agents with some information, or a threshold set elsewhere, change both limits; the simulations of T15 and T17 cover those cases |
| Human energy is counted in settlements: one owner settling one disagreement at a time, each an exponential time within the window, as in Part 1's law of triviality | Part 1's decision window (T27) and triviality; a counted proxy for attention, never a physiological measure | A team that settles disagreements in parallel spends the same settlements in less time; the mesh still grows with the square of the holders |
| In the unrouted protocol people judge each piece alone first; the machines' reference is recorded after; a holder who agrees with it costs nothing; a dispute is resolved to the truth with the verified share of Part 3; the machines miss a flaw that is a local fact | Proposition 8 (T39); constants from Parts 1 to 3, each changed alone in T39 | Disputes resolved less reliably cost better designs; a reference heard first is a leader that captures (Proposition 7) |
| Instruments from one source (one model, however prompted) err through a shared latent factor; instruments from different model sources share a smaller one; instruments grounded in a check against the system (tests, telemetry, static analysis), run by a person or an agent, err through the check's coverage, independently | Definition 6, following The Familiarity Bias (persona framing does not decorrelate shared weights) and The Shared Ancestor Problem (the layers of diversity) | T35 and T36 sweep the correlations from 0 to 0.9 |
| The latent correlation of roles of one model is near 0.6 | Chosen so the right-or-wrong correlation it implies at an error of 0.25, 0.384, matches the judge correlations Hossain and coauthors measured: 0.36 to 0.39 between two runs of one model, 0.42 across frontier providers | Judges on preference tasks stand in for review instruments; the sweep shows the direction at every value |
| A debate with an evidence channel (tests, telemetry, the running system) is right 0.9 of the time and independent of the model's blind spots | Illustrative, after Khan and coauthors, whose debaters could read evidence the judges could not | A channel that the model also reads only partly escapes its blind spots |
| Accuracy and correlation are separate settings: probes of one's own can raise a holder's precision without raising the correlation of holders' errors | Model choice (T40); the direction of shared briefings follows Proposition 5 | If every probe leans on one source, accuracy and correlation rise together and the probe step returns what T40's briefing rows show; prediction F22 |
| Routed holders of a piece's fact catch its flaw 0.9 of the time, independently of each other; holders chosen at random catch it at Part 2's informed share, 0.4 | Illustrative (T41); routing assumes the holders can be named | Holders nobody can name turn routing into the random case, which adopts worse designs far more often; holders who share a history share its blind spots, and two of them count for less than two, as Part 1's correlation says |
| The machines list each piece with one chance whatever it hides, and only holders of the fact add a missed flaw | Proposition 6's blind spot, the hardest case (T42) | Machines whose doubt tracks their blind spots need fewer additions; holders who add questions at random cost settlements without finding the flaw; prediction F23 |
| Approvers' stated priorities are ideal points in a plane with Euclidean preferences; an amendment is the nearest point every gate strictly prefers to the status quo | Proposition 3's spatial model (T43) | Unstated or non-spatial priorities leave no amendment to compute |
| The attention figure reads adoption as how often a review decides right when half the designs it sees are better: the mean of adopting a better design and rejecting a worse one | Presentation choice (T41), so that one linear number replaces the pair of adoption rates | With mostly better designs a review that adopts everything looks better, and with mostly worse ones a review that rejects everything does: split into pieces, the meeting adopts fewer worse designs than the protocol because it adopts almost nothing. Half and half is the mix at which neither shortcut scores above a coin flip |
